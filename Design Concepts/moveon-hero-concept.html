Hero concept

The Assembly

A homepage opening where a MoveOn scooter builds itself in front of the visitor, then rolls into a bright showroom. Every model is one click away there, and every model leads to Book a test drive.

MoveOn EV · Moto

Business goal
More test-drive bookings

The idea

One continuous shot, no loading screen

Most sites show a spinner, then cut to the page. Here the loading time is the brand moment. The scooter that assembles while the page loads becomes the first model in the hero, so the visitor never sees a hard cut. The website builds itself around the product.

Brand intro · 3 s

A dark stage, the MoveOn wordmark, and the scooter assembling under a single spotlight. It feels theatrical and unmistakably the brand.

Showroom · the rest of the visit

The lights come on and open into a bright studio. It's calm and premium, so the product and the price do the selling.

Assemble→Reveal→Select→Explore→Book a test drive

Scene 1 · 0.0 – 2.5 s · Brand intro

The pen draws, the parts dock

The screen is dark and full-bleed: no navbar and no buttons, just the wordmark and a soft spotlight. First, two construction lines set up the page, the ground and the centre axis. Then a glowing pen traces the scooter's whole silhouette in one continuous stroke, so the visitor sees its shape before anything moves. The base fills in inside that outline, and the other nine parts glide a short way in and dock into their slots, each drawn as blueprint lines traced from the real scooter.

Assembly order

The big base never flies. It's drawn in place, so every other part has a visible slot to dock into, and the headlamps go last. This keeps the build looking like engineering instead of objects floating around.

    Scene 2 · The lock

    Every part clicks into place

    Each part moves, overshoots its spot slightly, settles, and locks. When it locks, a small ring pulses at the joint and the whole frame dips by about 3 px, like a component being seated. That weight is what makes the build feel mechanical.

    Scene 3 · 2.5 – 5.15 s · Scan & hand-off

    Hold, scan, then the showroom lights come on

    Once the blueprint is complete, a scan line sweeps down it and the real photo develops over the drawing, top to bottom. The headlamps flick on and the scooter holds for half a second, long enough for the visitor to register "it just built itself." Then a circle of light opens outward from the scooter and turns the dark stage into a bright studio. At the same time the scooter rolls to the right two-thirds of the screen and grows to its hero size, and the website forms around it.

    Scene 4 · The showroom

    Hero layout

    The model selector takes the left third and the product takes the right two-thirds. The scooter is big, at about 70% of the screen height, facing the visitor at a front-¾ angle like a studio product shot. A huge, very faint tagline sits behind it as texture. The scooter is always the foreground object.

    1. NavbarThe wordmark from the intro settles here.
    2. Scooter selectorNumbered model list. The active model gets an underline. It stays minimal so the product holds focus.
    3. Giant taglineVery faint text behind the scooter that changes with each model.
    4. Hero scooterFront-¾ view at about 70% of viewport height, with a subtle idle float.
    5. Contact shadowA soft shadow on the studio floor that breathes with the float.
    6. Price tagA clean white card beside the handlebars. It pops in, then floats gently.
    7. Key specsRange, top speed and charge time.
    8. Book a test driveThe only brand-green element on the page. It isn't "Buy now" or "Learn more", because the goal is getting people into the showroom.
    9. Studio backdropA soft grey disc and floor gradient, like a photo studio sweep.

    Built as layers

    Five planes, each animated on its own

    Keeping each plane separate lets us move the scooter without touching the text, swap the tagline behind a scooter that stays put, and keep the interface sharp while everything behind it moves. It also means prices, names and buttons are real text that search engines and screen readers can read, not text baked into images.

    Scene 5 · Switching models

    The scooters glide through the showroom

    Clicking another model never just swaps the image. The current scooter slides out, shrinking slightly as it moves away, and the new one slides in with a touch of motion blur. The direction follows the list: a later model enters from the right and an earlier one from the left, so the lineup feels like a real row of scooters in a physical space.

    Everything about the model changes, not just the picture

    The tagline, price, specs, underline and dots each move on their own, sliding out and back in the direction of travel. That makes the whole hero feel like it's changing product, not just loading a new photo.

    • 0 msCurrent scooter speeds up and leaves. Text slides out.
    • 350 msNew scooter starts rolling in while the old one is still leaving.
    • 450 msName, tagline, price and specs swap while hidden.
    • 550 msNew text slides in from the side the scooter came from.
    • 1450 msScooter settles, shadow returns and the selector accepts clicks again.

    Details

    Small things that make it feel alive

    Breathing idle

    The scooter floats 4–5 px on a slow 3.6 s loop, and its shadow narrows as it rises. Visitors shouldn't consciously notice it, just feel that the product is alive. (Motion exaggerated above for clarity.)

    Floating price tag

    It scales in from 80% with a slight overshoot, then drifts gently. The price changes with a quick slide whenever the model changes.

    Default·Hover

    The one button that matters

    It's the only brand-green element in the hero. On hover, it fills with ink from the left, the arrow steps forward and a second arrow follows it. Hover over the top button to try it.

    On a phone

    Same story, stacked

    A front-¾ scooter is taller than it is wide, so it fits a portrait phone screen naturally. The intro plays with the same parts and order, but the parts travel shorter distances. In the hero, the scooter sits in the centre and the model list becomes a row of chips underneath it. Swiping left or right on the scooter switches models with the same slide-through motion.

    • Book a test drive is a full-width button within easy reach of the thumb.
    • The tagline shrinks but still sits behind the scooter.
    • The intro is shorter on mobile (about 3.5 s), and swiping works from the first frame of the hero.

    How we'll build it

    Custom Next.js + GSAP, not a template

    The intro and the hero run on one timeline, so the scooter can travel from its assembly point to its hero position while the showroom lights come on around it. A Framer or template build can't do this. It needs the page and the animation to share the same objects.

    Performance

    • We only animate position, scale, opacity and two clip-paths (the scan and the light), which keeps the motion at 60 fps even on mid-range Android phones.
    • The intro plays once per visit. Returning visitors go straight to the hero, and anyone can skip the intro.
    • Part layers are SVG where possible and compressed WebP/AVIF otherwise. The next model loads in the background when someone hovers over its name.
    • GSAP runs through useGSAP(), so animations are cleaned up properly when visitors navigate around the Next.js app.

    Accessibility & SEO

    • Visitors who turn on "reduce motion" skip the assembly. The showroom fades in and model changes crossfade instead.
    • The model list uses real buttons, so arrow keys switch models and the focus outline is always visible.
    • Model names, prices, specs and the call to action are real HTML text that Google and screen readers can read.
    • When the model changes, screen readers announce it quietly.

    What we need from you

    To make it real

    • Layered product art for each model, at a front-¾ angle. This is the angle shown throughout this document. Each scooter needs to be split into the ten parts listed in Scene 2, all from the same camera position. The blueprint lines in this document were traced automatically from a reference photo. With your CAD files, they can be your real engineering drawings. 3D/CAD files are ideal. Otherwise, a studio shoot planned around separating the parts.
    • Model line-up and order. This sets the left-to-right direction of the showroom.
    • Price, range, top speed and charge time for each model.
    • Taglines for each model, or approval of the drafts we suggest.
    • Brand guidelines, especially a version of the logo for light backgrounds.
    • Where "Book a test drive" should lead: a booking form, a showroom locator or WhatsApp.
